Ranked XI · The US soccer pyramid

The US soccer pyramid, ranked

Every tier, with the median rating of each league · 2,982 rated clubs · ratings computed 23 August 2026 · method last changed August 1, 2026

American soccer has no promotion or relegation, so its "pyramid" is a set of sanctioned divisions plus a much larger amateur and college game that sits outside them. Ranked XI rates 2,982 clubs (2,093 men’s, 889 women’s) across 40 leagues on a single Elo-style scale, so the tiers below can be compared by median rating rather than by where they sit on a chart. Cross-league placement is calibrated on roughly 600 U.S. Open Cup results. Updated 23 August 2026.

How the tiers are compared

Within a league, ratings are evidence: they come from results or standings. Between leagues they are measured — league anchors come from roughly 600 cross-league U.S. Open Cup results across the last five editions, the only competition where clubs from different tiers actually play each other. On top of that anchor, a club's own Cup results move its own rating.

Club Coefficient scores men's clubs by multi-year honors and league strength. Ranked XI puts men's and women's, college included, on one current Elo-style scale, and labels whether each rating is real results, real standings, an independent model, or illustrative.

Common questions

What is the US soccer pyramid?
It is the structure of American league soccer: U.S. Soccer sanctions Division I, II and III for professional men's leagues, and Division I for professional women's leagues. Below and alongside those sit national amateur leagues, regional leagues, and the college game. There is no promotion or relegation between them.
Which league is the top division in the United States?
MLS is Division I for men. NWSL is Division I for women. USL Championship is Division II for men, and USL League One and MLS Next Pro are Division III.
How can college soccer be compared to professional leagues?
College teams never play in the U.S. Open Cup, so no real cross-league results anchor them. Ranked XI orders college divisions by independent Massey ratings and places them in calibrated bands — men's below the USL Championship floor, women's higher, because college is the women's game's primary development tier.
Is a higher tier always a stronger club?
No. The tables below show median ratings, and the ranges overlap. A strong national-amateur club can rate above a weak Division III professional side, which is what Open Cup results show when those clubs actually meet.
